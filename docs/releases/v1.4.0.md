# v1.4.0 — The Duelist

This release is mostly about **how the ship moves** and **what moves against it**.

Steering has been rebuilt: the rubber-band lag is gone, the ship now obeys one
motion law across a whole gesture instead of switching mid-flick, and — because
mouse hardware varies several-fold and no browser will tell us by how much —
**sensitivity is now a setting you control**, in the Menu and mid-run from the
Pause overlay.

Against that, a new enemy. The **Duelist** arrives at wave 35 and is the first
thing in the game that answers *where you are*. Everything else flies a path
decided when it spawned. The Duelist picks a firing position off your position,
commits to it, flies there, shoots, and picks another — over and over, nose
tracking you the whole time.

The **Lode** also gets its own art and a retune around the smaller rock.

## Highlights

- **Steering no longer rubber-bands.** The ship chases a target point, and that
  target could previously run arbitrarily far ahead on a fast flick — so on a
  direction reversal it had to travel all that debt back before the ship visibly
  turned. How far it may lead is now **capped**, so the debt is bounded however
  violent the gesture.
- **One motion law, not two.** The old top-speed clamp kicked in on any decent
  flick and switched the ship from eased motion to a flat rate and back. That
  discontinuity — mid-gesture — is what read as inconsistency. It is gone; speed
  now falls out of the easing itself.
- **Sensitivity is yours.** A new **Sensitivity** row sits alongside Music and SFX,
  adjustable from **25% to 300%** in 25% steps, persisted between runs, and
  changeable **mid-run from the Pause overlay**. Mouse DPI varies about fourfold
  across common hardware and no web API exposes it, so this is the only way the
  game can feel the same in your hand as in someone else's.
- **Hand motion now maps to the world, not the window.** Resizing the window no
  longer changes how far the ship travels per unit of hand motion — muscle memory
  survives a resize.
- **Engine now genuinely makes you faster.** It multiplies whatever sensitivity you
  set (reaching **×2.25 at cap for everyone**, whatever your slider) and raises top
  speed from roughly **1500 to 2080 px/s** across its 10 stacks.
- **The Duelist — the first enemy that answers your position.** From **wave 35**,
  a light, quick ship that sets up above you, keeps its nose pointed at you at all
  times, and repeats one loop: **fly to a spot, fire on arrival, hold, pick a new
  spot.** At most two are ever on the field.
- **Its shots follow its nose, and its nose can be outrun.** Easing out of the line
  does not work — the nose tracks you. A committed lateral break does: the nose
  falls behind and the later shots of the volley trail into empty space. The
  facing is an honest read on where the next bullet goes.
- **The Lode gets real art.** It is no longer a tinted, shimmering asteroid — it is
  its own sprite, and smaller.

## Details

### Steering

- **The lead cap.** The steer target may now sit at most **88px** ahead of the ship.
  Because ship velocity is proportional to that lead, bounding it bounds the top
  speed too — through the easing rather than through a separate clamp. The old
  `maxSpeed` teleport-guard is deleted.
- **What you will feel.** Direction reversals bite immediately instead of after a
  wind-back. Long traversals are unchanged in pace (`followResponse` rose 12 → 20
  to hold traversal speed under the tighter cap — the two are coupled and must be
  tuned together).
- **A note on refresh rate.** Top speed is now **frame-rate dependent**: about
  1500 px/s at 60Hz, somewhat faster at 144Hz or 240Hz. The old clamp was
  frame-rate independent, so this is a real, accepted regression — judged worth it
  for having one motion law across the whole gesture instead of switching mid-flick.
- **Raw input, disclosed.** Chrome grants raw, unaccelerated pointer input; Firefox
  and Safari reject it and apply the OS pointer-acceleration curve, which no API
  exposes. The game now **records whether raw input was granted and says so in the
  settings UI**, rather than leaving you to wonder why the same sensitivity value
  feels different in another browser.
- **Engine went multiplicative** as a direct consequence of sensitivity becoming a
  setting. An additive bonus against a player-chosen base warps wildly — at a 0.2
  setting a fixed +1.0 is a 6× boost, at 2.4 it is barely 1.4× — so the upgrade's
  power would have depended on your mouse. It now multiplies, holding ×2.25 at cap
  for every player.

### The Duelist

- **Where and when.** Enters the normal spawn budget from **wave 35** at a small
  flat share, alongside everything else the endgame throws at you.
- **The loop.** It picks a spot roughly **310–390px** from you in the band above,
  eases there (accelerating away, decelerating in), fires a **3-shot burst the
  instant it arrives**, holds the spot for **1.6s**, then picks the next one — a
  little further around the arc than the last, reversing at the ends, so it paces
  across you rather than circling.
- **It commits.** The spot is chosen off where you are *at that instant* and then
  frozen. It flies to the spot it picked, not to where you have since gone — so
  moving right after it commits **pulls it out of position** for that whole beat.
  That is the counterplay, and it only exists because the destination is fixed.
- **The burst tracks, within limits.** Each shot leaves along the current nose, and
  the nose turns at a fixed rate. Drifting sideways is tracked and punished.
  Committing to a hard break (roughly 800–1000 px/s, against your ~1500 px/s top
  speed) outruns it, and shots two and three land wide.
- **It keeps its distance from your hull, not your gun.** It can sit directly above
  you, and lining up under it to fire straight up is exactly how it dies. What the
  standoff protects is against being rammed — no spot is ever *chosen* on top of
  you. But because the spot is frozen, you can charge a committed Duelist and reach
  it. You will take contact damage for it.
- **Never more than two.** A Duelist never leaves the field, and a wave cannot clear
  until every one is dead — so the live count is hard-capped at **two** rather than
  left to a spawn chance. Two is deliberate: they open on **opposite sides**, so you
  take crossfire from two angles and can only line up under one at a time.
- **It is the first enemy drawn rotated.** Its HP Bar stays horizontal above it.

### The Lode

- **Its own sprite.** It shipped in v1.3.0 as frame 1 of the Asteroids sheet tinted
  gold and pulsing between two golds. It now has dedicated art, drawn untinted with
  the shimmer removed — **the colour lives in the pixels**.
- **A Lode and a Mine are now the same rock in different hues**, at the same 72×64
  size. That is deliberate: hue alone separates treasure from bomb.
- **Retuned around the smaller rock.** Shrinking it 160px → 72px cut its collision
  radius from 62px to 25px, which made the lead you must aim off in the top lane
  *larger than the hit window*. So: **HP 2000 → 1400**, and the crossing slowed from
  16s to **20s** (~100 px/s) to bring the required lead back inside the window. It
  is no longer at Boss HP parity — though its XP and Score still are, because they
  reward beating the deadline, not the size of the pool.
- **The drip halved in frequency** (every 3.2s rather than 1.6s) so the longer
  window does not inflate the consolation payout past the burst worth chasing.
- **Star burst speeds halved**, settling ~70–125px from the death point against the
  128px Kill Burst — a change ADR-0022 had deferred.
- The wave-15 damage check now asks about **188 sustained DPS**, and ~356 at wave 30.

## Under the hood

- **Two new Pixi-free math modules, both directly unit-tested.** `game/steering.ts`
  holds the lead cap and the frame-rate-independent easing; `game/duelist.ts` holds
  the standoff geometry, the arc stepping, the nose turn-rate limiter and the move
  interpolation. The test suite grows from 11 to **61 tests**.
- **The tests earned their keep on the Duelist.** Two load-bearing properties were
  wrong in the first implementation, and neither is visible by reading the code:
  clamping a chosen spot into the band let a player who flew up there drag it onto
  themselves, and the nose turn rate was set *above* the maximum a player at top
  speed could even demand — which silently made the burst undodgeable. Finding
  either by playing would have meant reaching wave 35 first.
- **The Duelist's live cap is enforced at spawn time**, not at wave composition. The
  wave queue is built up front, before anything is live, so composition physically
  cannot see the field; an over-cap slot pays out a Gunner instead so the wave keeps
  its full enemy budget.
- Audio and settings UI were refactored: the stepper row moved out of `AudioControls`
  into a reusable `SettingsControls` block now carrying Music, SFX and Sensitivity.
- New design records:
  [ADR-0023](../adr/0023-configurable-sensitivity-and-lead-capped-steering.md) —
  which amends ADR-0006 — covers the lead cap, the deleted `maxSpeed`, world-space
  deltas, and why sensitivity had to become a setting and Engine had to become
  multiplicative. [ADR-0024](../adr/0024-duelist-player-relative-standoff.md) covers
  the Duelist: committed spots over continuous tracking, spending the band clamp
  sideways, and the geometry window that bounds the turn rate from both directions.

## Known tuning notes

- **The Duelist's balance is unplaytested at its real wave.** 130 base HP works out
  to roughly 765 effective at wave 35, sitting between a Warden and a SpaceStation,
  and it has not been tested against a real wave-35 build. `dwellSeconds` is the
  main difficulty knob after `hp` — it is the window you get to line up under it.
- **It now sets up close.** At 310–390px it is much nearer your zone than the
  original 620–770px, so contact damage is a live risk rather than theoretical, and
  its bullets reach you faster — which makes the burst harder independently of the
  dodge maths.
- **`turnRate` is coupled to `standoffRadius` and must be re-derived if that moves.**
  The window that keeps the burst dodgeable *and* punishing narrows as the Duelist
  gets closer; at the current distance it is roughly 1.3–3.2 rad/s.
- **Top speed now varies with refresh rate.** Worth watching whether high-refresh
  players find the ship too loose.
- `EnemyPool.ts` is now past 1600 lines with every enemy kind's state flattened into
  one class. It wants an extraction; that is deliberately not in this release.
